# Database coverage — which database for which field (2026)

No single database covers a field completely. This reference maps the major academic databases and their coverage as of 2026-07-31.

## Health and biomedical

**PubMed** ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/))
- ~37M records
- Free, comprehensive for biomedical / life sciences
- MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) enables precise search
- Free full text via PubMed Central for many articles
- **Use for:** any clinical, biological, health-services, or public-health review

**Embase** ([embase.com](https://www.embase.com/))
- ~40M records
- Subscription (institutional access typically required)
- Stronger European coverage than PubMed
- Emtree (Elsevier's controlled vocabulary)
- **Use for:** clinical and pharma reviews — often required by systematic-review methodological standards alongside PubMed

**Cochrane CENTRAL** (via [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/))
- Focused on RCTs
- Includes trial registrations and conference abstracts
- **Use for:** RCT-focused reviews as a required source

**ClinicalTrials.gov** and **ISRCTN**
- Trial registries — includes unpublished trials
- **Use for:** identifying registered trials with no published results (a source of publication bias)

**PsycINFO**
- APA-produced, psychology and behavioral sciences
- **Use for:** psychology, psychiatry, behavior-change interventions

## Broad science / multidisciplinary

**Web of Science** ([webofknowledge.com](https://www.webofscience.com/))
- Multidisciplinary
- Subscription
- Strong citation tracking (forward/backward citation)
- Selective indexing (higher-tier journals emphasized)
- **Use for:** citation searches, multi-disciplinary reviews

**Scopus** ([scopus.com](https://www.scopus.com/))
- Elsevier-produced, multidisciplinary
- Subscription
- Broader coverage than Web of Science, less selective
- Citation tracking
- **Use for:** broad-coverage searches, engineering + science mix

**Google Scholar** ([scholar.google.com](https://scholar.google.com/))
- Free
- Broadest coverage, including grey literature and books
- **Reliability caveats:** pagination is unreliable, results are not reproducible between sessions, no clean export
- **Use as:** a completeness check to catch papers missed by structured databases — never as the *only* database

## Computer science / engineering

**ACM Digital Library** ([dl.acm.org](https://dl.acm.org/))
- Association for Computing Machinery
- Strong CS coverage — SIGGRAPH, CHI, KDD, etc.
- Subscription

**IEEE Xplore** ([ieeexplore.ieee.org](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/))
- IEEE conferences and journals
- Engineering, ML, signal processing, communications
- Subscription

**DBLP** ([dblp.org](https://dblp.org/))
- Free CS bibliography
- Good for author-based search
- Does not include full text

## Preprint servers (SEPARATE TIER)

Preprints are not peer-reviewed. Include them in the review but tag them and flag when a preprint has since been peer-reviewed.

- **arXiv** ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/)) — math, CS, physics, statistics, quantitative biology
- **bioRxiv** ([biorxiv.org](https://www.biorxiv.org/)) — biology
- **medRxiv** ([medrxiv.org](https://www.medrxiv.org/)) — clinical / health
- **SSRN** ([ssrn.com](https://www.ssrn.com/)) — social sciences, business, law
- **ChemRxiv** — chemistry

## Grey literature (SEPARATE TIER)

Grey literature is unpublished or non-peer-reviewed reports. Include in the review but treat separately.

- Government reports (agency websites, OECD, WHO)
- Industry white papers
- Working papers (NBER, IZA, etc.)
- Conference abstracts not indexed in main databases
- Dissertations (ProQuest Dissertations & Theses)

## Field-specific databases

- **CINAHL** — nursing and allied health
- **ERIC** — education
- **EconLit** — economics
- **AGRICOLA** — agriculture
- **GeoRef** — earth sciences
- **INSPEC** — physics, EE, CS

## Rule for the reviewer

- **Use at least 2 field-specific databases** for a systematic review. One is not defensible.
- **Add Google Scholar as a completeness check**, not a primary source.
- **Include a preprint server** for any recent-methods review — the field moves before peer review completes.
- **Include grey literature** for policy or applied reviews — the pertinent evidence often lives outside peer-reviewed journals.
- **Log every database searched**, the query used, the date, and the result count.

## Special note — LLM benchmark contamination (2024–2026)

For ML reviews of LLM capabilities: many benchmarks published before ~2024 are contaminated in training data of models released after that date. When reviewing LLM benchmark results, note the benchmark's release date vs the model's training cutoff. Benchmark scores on contaminated benchmarks are not reliable measures of the model's generalization. See recent 2025–2026 papers on benchmark contamination for the current picture.
