# Example — Brokerage fee dispute (transaction closed, dispute open)

**Situation.** A secondary of pre-IPO stock closed cleanly. Six weeks later a second broker surfaces claiming they were the "procuring cause" and are owed a co-brokerage split. The primary broker denies. The seller is caught in the middle. No securities are at risk; only the fee allocation is disputed.

**Deliverable target.** A dispute memo (Recipe 5 from HOWTO.md), not the standing two-page transaction memo.

---

## Title
Vector Systems Secondary — Fee Dispute Memo

## What each side claims

**Broker P (primary of record):** Broker P was engaged by Seller S under a signed engagement letter dated 2026-03-11 with a 4% fee on gross purchase price. The transaction closed 2026-05-22 at $4,500,000 gross, generating a $180,000 fee. Broker P asserts they were solely responsible for sourcing Buyer B and that no other broker had a contractual or procuring-cause relationship with either party.

**Broker Q (claimant):** Broker Q asserts they introduced Buyer B to the transaction on 2026-02-27, before Broker P's engagement letter was signed, and are therefore the procuring cause. Broker Q demands a 50/50 co-brokerage split ($90,000). Broker Q has no written engagement with Seller S and no written co-brokerage agreement with Broker P.

## What each side's stated basis is

**Broker P's basis (per email 2026-07-01 and attached engagement letter):** Signed exclusive engagement letter with Seller S dated 2026-03-11, which pre-dates Broker Q's first documented outreach to Seller S (2026-03-19). Broker P also produced an email chain 2026-03-11 through 2026-05-22 showing continuous engagement with Buyer B's counsel, with no reference to Broker Q.

**Broker Q's basis (per demand letter 2026-07-05):** A single email dated 2026-02-27 from Broker Q to Buyer B's principal describing Vector Systems as a "compelling secondary opportunity" and offering to make an introduction to a potential seller. Broker Q asserts this email is the procuring cause under the industry standard for co-brokerage claims.

## What documents support each side

Supporting Broker P:
- Executed engagement letter with Seller S, 2026-03-11 (4% exclusive)
- Email from Seller S to Broker P, 2026-03-11, confirming exclusivity
- Continuous email chain Broker P ↔ Buyer B's counsel, 2026-03-14 through 2026-05-22
- Closing statement signed by Buyer B and Seller S, 2026-05-22, naming Broker P as sole broker of record

Supporting Broker Q:
- Email from Broker Q to Buyer B's principal, 2026-02-27, mentioning "Vector Systems secondary opportunity"

## What documents contradict each side

Contradicting Broker Q:
- Buyer B's principal's reply to the 2026-02-27 email, dated 2026-02-28, stating: "We are already aware of Vector — thanks for the note but no need to make an introduction." (This weakens the procuring-cause claim materially.)
- Broker Q's own email log shows no further outreach on Vector Systems between 2026-02-28 and 2026-06-15 (when the closing became publicly known).
- No engagement letter, no communication with Seller S, no communication with Buyer B's counsel during the 2026-03-11 to 2026-05-22 transaction window.

Contradicting Broker P:
- None material. Broker P's paper trail is complete.

## What evidence would resolve the dispute

The dispute would be resolved by one of the following:

1. A written co-brokerage agreement between Broker P and Broker Q dated before 2026-05-22. **Neither side has produced one and both sides deny one exists.** This is close to dispositive against Broker Q.
2. Testimony or written correspondence from Buyer B's principal or Buyer B's counsel indicating that the 2026-02-27 email materially influenced their decision to engage with Vector. **The 2026-02-28 reply above already contradicts this.**
3. A prior course of dealing between Broker P and Broker Q that established an implied fee-split for referrals. **Neither side has raised this.**

## Attribution

- "Broker Q was the procuring cause" — allegation by Broker Q; contested by Broker P.
- "Broker P had a signed exclusive engagement" — established by the executed engagement letter.
- "Buyer B was aware of Vector before Broker Q's email" — established by Buyer B's 2026-02-28 reply.
- "A 50/50 co-brokerage split is owed" — allegation by Broker Q; no contractual basis in the record.

## Bottom line for the memo reader

On the record supplied, the securities transaction closed cleanly and is not at issue. The fee dispute is between two intermediaries, not between buyer and seller. The record supports Broker P's exclusive engagement claim and contradicts Broker Q's procuring-cause claim on the specific facts. The reader should regard Broker Q's demand as weak on the current record but should not treat the matter as closed absent either a settlement or a formal release from Broker Q.
